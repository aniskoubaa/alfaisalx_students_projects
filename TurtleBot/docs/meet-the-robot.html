01

What it is and why the lab has it

This is a small wheeled robot, about the size of a large pizza box lying flat. It has a laser scanner that measures distances, a camera, a small computer, a display and a battery, and it can drive itself back to its charging dock. The lab uses it to teach students how robots sense the world and move, using ROS 2, the software that most research robots run.

  • Learn ROS 2Topics, services, actions and nodes on a real machine.
  • Read sensorsLidar, wheel odometry, IMU, battery, bump and cliff sensors.
  • Move on its ownUndock, drive a shape, avoid a wall, dock again.
  • Map and navigateThe next step after the basics. Not yet tested on this robot.
For lab students: the hardware in one paragraph

The lab has a TurtleBot 4 Standard: a Raspberry Pi 4 on an iRobot Create 3 base, running Ubuntu 24.04 Server and ROS 2 Jazzy. The Pi runs the lidar (an RPLIDAR), the OAK-D camera and the display. The Create 3 base runs its own firmware (I.0.0.FastDDS) and handles the wheels, bump, cliff and dock sensors, the light ring and the battery. The robot has no desktop: you work on it from your own computer over SSH. Start with Do it yourself or HOW-TO-CONNECT.md. The longer hardware overview is the field guide.

1. The base (iRobot Create 3)

The round platform that drives. It is the same kind of base as a robot vacuum, with wheels, a bumper and sensors that stop it from falling off a table edge.

Parts
Two drive wheels, a bumper, cliff sensors under the front, infrared sensors that find the dock, an IMU, the light ring and the battery. Per the field guide: differential drive, wheel encoders, bump, cliff and IR sensors, speaker.
ROS 2
/cmd_vel (drive; type TwistStamped), /odom (wheel odometry), /imu, /wheel_status, /hazard_detection (bump and cliff), /ir_opcode (what the IR sensors see). Action /rotate_angle.
Try it
Read only, safe on the dock: python3 sensor_report.py --seconds 20 (rates of /odom, /imu and more). Moving programs: examples/README.md, safety checklist first.
More for students

The base is a separate computer with its own firmware (I.0.0.FastDDS). The Pi re-publishes its topics with create3_republisher. The base web page is at http://192.168.186.2 from the Pi. Known issue: /odom can pause for about a second or more when the wheels start; motion_shapes.py holds still and waits. See status below and motion program design.

2. Raspberry Pi 4

The robot's small computer. It reads the lidar and camera, runs the display and talks to your laptop over Wi-Fi.

Does
Runs Ubuntu 24.04 Server and ROS 2 Jazzy (no desktop). Starts the lidar, camera and display software, and re-publishes the base's topics. You log in to it with SSH and run your programs on it.
ROS 2
Nodes seen on 2026-10-04: /rplidar_composition, /turtlebot4_base_node, /oakd, /turtlebot4_diagnostics. Topic /ip holds its current address.
Try it
ssh ubuntu@turtlebot4.local
If the name is not found, use the address on the robot's display. Open a terminal first: PowerShell on Windows, Terminal on macOS or Linux. Password: ask the lab maintainer. Full steps: Do it yourself.
More for students

The Pi talks to the base over a USB network link: the Pi is 192.168.186.3, the base is 192.168.186.2. Do not unplug that cable. Programs live in ~/robot_code. The robot's clock can be wrong because the campus network blocks internet time; see HOW-TO-CONNECT.md.

3. Lidar (RPLIDAR)

A spinning laser that measures how far away the nearest object is in every direction. It is how the robot "sees" walls and chairs.

Does
Measured on 2026-10-04: about 7.75 scans per second, 720 beams per scan, range 0.15 m to 12 m. The lidar is mounted turned by 90 degrees: straight ahead of the robot is laser angle -90 degrees. It stops while the robot is docked and starts after undock (power saving, expected).
ROS 2
/scan, type sensor_msgs/msg/LaserScan. Subscribe with qos_profile_sensor_data or no messages arrive.
Try it
Off the dock: 
python3 scan_test.py
Prints the nearest object in metres. Guide with an interactive scan explorer: lidar-guide.html. How a lidar fault was fixed: lidar-diagnosis.html.
More for students

After an undock the lidar may stay stopped; see Known issues in MAINTENANCE.md. python3 clearance_check.py 0.6 reads one scan and exits 0 if the way ahead is clear. python3 lidar_snapshot.py saves scans to a file you can draw on any computer.

4. Camera (OAK-D)

A depth camera with two lenses, like two eyes, plus a normal colour lens. It can measure distance and recognise objects.

Does
The field guide describes the OAK-D as stereo depth plus RGB, with an onboard processor for AI. The repo does not say whether this robot has the Pro or the Lite version. The camera software runs on the Pi and uses about one CPU core.
ROS 2
The node /oakd exists. The field guide lists its topics as /oakd/*. The exact image topics were not recorded for this robot [to verify].
Try it
List what the camera publishes: 
ros2 topic list | grep oakd
[to verify]. No example program in this repo uses the camera yet. Streaming raw images over Wi-Fi is slow; the roadmap advises compressed topics.

5. Display and buttons

A small screen on the robot. It shows the robot's network address and can show a message from a program.

Does
After switching on, the robot plays a chime and its address appears on the display. You need that address to connect.
ROS 2
Text goes to /hmi/display/message (type std_msgs/msg/String).
Try it
Safe anywhere, also on the dock: 
python3 hello_robot.py
Shows a message on the display and turns the light ring green for 5 s. See examples/README.md.
More for students

The buttons are not used by any program in this repo, and their topic is not recorded in the repo docs [to verify]. The base has its own power button in the centre of the light ring; press and hold it for about 7 to 10 s to turn the robot off (see HOW-TO-CONNECT.md).

6. Light ring

A ring of coloured lights on top of the base. It shows the robot's state, like a status lamp.

Does
Six LEDs. A program can take control of them by setting override_system.
ROS 2
/cmd_lightring, type irobot_create_msgs/msg/LightringLeds.
Try it
python3 lightring_status.py
Shows the battery level and a blinking red ring if the lidar data is stale, for a set time, then hands the ring back. Details: examples/README.md.

7. Battery

The robot runs on a rechargeable battery. When it gets low, it can drive back to its dock to charge.

Does
Powers the base and the Pi. The base reports its charge.
ROS 2
/battery_state, type sensor_msgs/msg/BatteryState.
Try it
timeout 60 ros2 topic echo --once /battery_state
Run on the robot. Every ros2 call takes 30 to 60 s on the Pi, so wait.
More for students

The repo does not record the battery capacity or the run time, so none is given here. python3 sensor_report.py --seconds 20 prints the battery along with the other topic rates.

8. Charging dock

The robot's charging station. It sends out infrared signals, and the robot follows them to park itself and charge.

Does
The robot finds the dock with two infrared sensors, lines up on the centre line in front of it and drives in slowly until the charging contacts touch. Undock and dock were verified on this robot (undock 2026-10-04, dock 2026-10-03).
ROS 2
Topic /dock_status (field is_docked), topic /ir_opcode, actions /dock and /undock.
Try it
Clear about 1 m around the robot first. This moves it:
ros2 action send_goal /undock irobot_create_msgs/action/Undock "{}"
Explained step by step, with an animation: docking-explained.html.
03

How the parts talk

Your laptop talks to the Pi over Wi-Fi (or an Ethernet cable). The Pi talks to the base over a USB cable that works like a tiny network. Every sensor and command is a ROS 2 topic that flows across these links. The longer story is in How a command travels.

04

What it can and cannot do yet

State on 2026-10-24, from README.md, MAINTENANCE.md and ROADMAP.md.

Works

  • Connect from Windows, macOS or Linux, over Wi-Fi or a direct Ethernet cable.
  • Undock and dock (verified 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-03).
  • Lidar publishes /scan off the dock (7.75 Hz on 2026-10-04).
  • Text on the display and the light ring from a program (verified 2026-10-03).
  • Supervised motion programs (spin, circle and other shapes) completed in the latest test: 10 of 10 motion starts, with short pauses at the start (ROADMAP TB-25).

Being fixed

  • Short /odom pauses when the wheels start. The cause is in the base, not the Wi-Fi or the Pi. Programs wait through them (TB-15, TB-25).
  • Wi-Fi drops since 2026-10-04. Power saving was switched off on 2026-10-24; whether that ends the drops can only be checked on campus (MAINTENANCE.md R14).
  • After an undock the lidar may stay stopped (Known issues in MAINTENANCE.md).

Not yet tested

  • Mapping and navigation (SLAM, Nav2) on this robot.
  • The camera in a program. Streaming images over Wi-Fi.
  • The display buttons.
  • TurtleBot Studio on macOS, Linux and over Wi-Fi.
