Four ways to get this wrong

Each of these will cost you hardware. Three of them look reasonable until you check.

× The 5‑pin balance lead is not a power source

It is a per-cell tap for the charger. Thin wire, and which pins you grab decides whether you get 3.7, 7.4, 11.1 or 14.8 V. Shorting adjacent pins damages cells. Use the main discharge lead — two thick wires, XT60 or XT30.

× The Pixhawk POWER port is an input

It takes 5 V into the flight controller from the power module, plus voltage and current sense lines. There is no 12 V rail to draw from, and it cannot feed a 12 W gimbal. It is a 6‑pin JST‑GH, so if you are counting five, you are looking at a different port.

× USB‑C will not power or stream the camera

On the A8 mini it exists for firmware upgrades via SIYI Assistant on Windows. It does not appear in the manual's port list, and it does not enumerate on Linux — an empty dmesg already confirmed that on your board.

× Do not run the camera through the PDB

SIYI is explicit: if the aircraft rolls hard, power the gimbal directly from a 3S–6S battery, not through a power distribution board or air unit. Give it its own branch off the main lead.

Verify this before buying a transmitter

The manual describes the camera's video output as a switchable mode — “switch video output mode to HDMI under the Gimbal Config page”, and “HDMI / CVBS video output is changed to dynamic switch”. If selecting HDMI also disables the Ethernet stream, then HDMI to the Jetson and Ethernet to the transmitter cannot run at the same time, and this harness does not work.

Test it on the bench: enable HDMI output, confirm the capture card sees a picture, then check whether rtsp://192.168.144.25:8554/video1 still opens. Ten minutes, and it decides the architecture.

Check your manufacturing date

A8 minis built before June 2023 may not accept 6S. If you are unsure which lot yours is from, stay on 3S or 4S. There is nothing to gain from testing it.

Every connection, listed

Nine links. Seven are required, two are optional and can wait.

FromToCarriesNotes
LiPo main lead XT60 splitter pack voltage required Everything branches from here. Balance lead goes nowhere but the charger.
Splitter Power module → POWER1 5 V + sensing required 6-pin JST-GH into the Pixhawk. The module steps down; the Pixhawk never sees pack voltage.
Splitter A8 mini power cable 11–25.2 V required Its own branch, straight off the main lead. 5 W average, 12 W peak — about 1 A at 12 V.
Splitter Video transmitter per its spec required Check the transmitter's input range before wiring. The capture card draws its power from the Jetson's USB port, so it needs no separate branch.
Splitter DC regulator → Jetson carrier-dependent required 5 V, 12 V or 19 V depending on the carrier board. Confirm before wiring — still an open question.
A8 mini micro-HDMI USB capture card 1080p video required Set the camera's video output mode to HDMI in SIYI Assistant first. 1080p is the ceiling on any live output.
Capture card Jetson USB 3.0 port UVC video required Appears as /dev/video0. Must be a USB 3.0 port — check lsusb -t reads 5000M, not just that the device shows up.
A8 mini Ethernet Video transmitter downlink video required Operator downlink straight from the camera. Carries no detection overlays.
A8 mini UART Jetson serial / USB-TTL gimbal control required SIYI SDK over serial. Replaces the UDP control path, since Ethernet now feeds the transmitter.
Pixhawk TELEM1 Jetson UART / USB MAVLink required MAVROS. Read-only — the Jetson consumes GPS and attitude and never sends control commands.
A8 mini UART Pixhawk TELEM2 attitude + MAVLink optional Improves gimbal stabilisation in fast turns, and is what lets the camera write GPS into photo EXIF. Cable is in the box. Add it after video works.
RC receiver S.Bus A8 mini control port manual pointing optional Pilot fallback for aiming the gimbal by hand. Independent of the Jetson.

If you add the optional UART

You then have two things that can command the gimbal — the Pixhawk over UART and the Jetson over Ethernet. They will fight if both try to point it. Pick one authority: Pixhawk supplies attitude data, Jetson does the pointing.

Bench build order

Do it in this order. Each step is testable before the next one adds a variable.

01
Use a bench supply, not a LiPo
Set 12 V, current limit 2 A. A wiring mistake trips the supply instead of melting something, and you cannot over-discharge a pack while debugging. Save the LiPo for the airframe.
02
Power the camera alone
Nothing else connected. Confirm the three-axis self-test sweep, then a solid green status LED. Yellow blinking means under 10 V.
03
Switch the camera to HDMI output
SIYI Assistant on a Windows PC, under Gimbal Config. Out of the box it may be set to Ethernet, in which case the HDMI port is silent and the capture card sees nothing.
04
Test the simultaneity question
With HDMI enabled, check whether rtsp://192.168.144.25:8554/video1 still opens. If it does not, the split HDMI/Ethernet harness is impossible and the plan needs rethinking. Do this before buying a transmitter.
05
Prove the capture path
lsusb -t (expect 5000M), then v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --list-formats-ext, then ffplay /dev/video0. Record the negotiated resolution, format and frame rate.
06
Then the Pixhawk link
TELEM1 to the Jetson, bring up MAVROS, confirm GPS and attitude are arriving as topics. Only now is the harness worth moving onto the airframe.

Still unanswered

Which carrier board is the Orin NX on? That sets the regulator output — 5 V, 12 V or 19 V — and it is the one number in this diagram still marked “to carrier spec”. Also worth confirming: is the Pixhawk 6C running ArduPilot or PX4, since the optional gimbal parameters differ.
