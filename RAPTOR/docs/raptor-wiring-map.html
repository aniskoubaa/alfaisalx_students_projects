What plugs into what

Two jobs, split cleanly. The Pixhawk points the gimbal through the A8 mini's control port, and the Jetson sees through it over micro‑HDMI into a capture card. The Ethernet stream to a VTX is a separate, optional path that gives the operator a picture without going through the Jetson at all. Two paths to the picture

The camera has three ports and they do three different jobs. Keeping them straight is most of what this page is for.

PortGoes toJob
Control port Pixhawk 6C Pointing. The flight controller owns the gimbal — it knows the aircraft's attitude and it already takes the pilot's stick input. Nothing else commands the mount. required
micro‑HDMI Capture card → Jetson Seeing. This is the perception feed. It never leaves the aircraft, so it does not compete with the downlink for radio bandwidth and does not care whether a VTX is fitted. required
Ethernet VTX → ground station Showing. A picture for the operator, straight off the camera. Independent of the Jetson, so a Jetson fault does not blind the pilot — but it carries no detection overlays. optional

What this costs the Jetson

Handing the gimbal to the Pixhawk means the Jetson cannot point the camera directly. If the perception stack wants to look at something — centre a detected person, sweep a sector — it has to ask, by sending a MAVLink gimbal command to the Pixhawk over TELEM1 and letting the flight controller carry it out.

That is a change of posture worth naming: the design docs currently say the Jetson reads telemetry and never writes. Autonomous pointing breaks that rule. Either the Jetson gets permission to write gimbal commands only — never anything that affects flight — or pointing stays entirely manual and the Jetson just watches wherever the pilot aims. Decide which before writing the gimbal node.

Where does the Jetson actually go?

The X500 V2 top deck is roughly 180 × 180 mm, and in the photos the Pixhawk and the R86C already take most of it. An Orin NX on a carrier board, plus a capture card, plus a USB hub, plus their cabling, is not going to drop into what is left.

This is a real open item, not a detail: it decides the buck converter's position, the length of the HDMI run, and the centre of gravity. Work out the mounting before buying the VTX — the answer may be a second deck above the FC, or a plate slung under the battery tray.

Four ways to get this wrong

Each of these will cost you hardware. Three of them look reasonable until you check.

× The 5‑pin balance lead is not a power source

It is a per-cell tap for the charger. Thin wire, and which pins you grab decides whether you get 3.7, 7.4, 11.1 or 14.8 V. Shorting adjacent pins damages cells. Use the main discharge lead — the EC5, through its XT60 adapter.

× The Pixhawk POWER port is an input

It takes 5 V into the flight controller from the power module, plus voltage and current sense lines. There is no 12 V rail to draw from, and it cannot feed a 12 W gimbal. It is a 6‑pin JST‑GH, so if you are counting five, you are looking at a different port.

× USB‑C will not power or stream the camera

On the A8 mini it exists for firmware upgrades via SIYI Assistant on Windows. It does not appear in the manual's port list, and it does not enumerate on Linux — an empty dmesg already confirmed that on your board.

× A buck converter is not a volt meter

Part 5 on the list was corrected in ink for a reason. An “adjustable volt meter” module displays voltage and passes it through unchanged — wire the Jetson behind one and it still sees full pack voltage. You want a step‑down (buck) converter, sized for the Orin NX at its highest nvpmodel mode plus headroom.

Give the camera its own branch

SIYI is explicit: if the aircraft rolls hard, power the gimbal directly from a 3S–6S battery, not through a power distribution board or an air unit. That is why the camera is drawn on its own XT30 branch here rather than hanging off the VTX — and it has the useful side effect that the camera works identically whether or not a VTX is fitted. 5 W average, 12 W peak, about 1 A at 12 V.

Before you buy the VTX, test the simultaneity question

The manual describes the camera's video output as a switchable mode — “switch video output mode to HDMI under the Gimbal Config page”, and “HDMI / CVBS video output is changed to dynamic switch”. If selecting HDMI also disables the Ethernet stream, then the downlink option simply does not exist alongside the Jetson feed, and there is no point buying a transmitter at all.

Test it on the bench: enable HDMI output, confirm the capture card sees a picture, then check whether rtsp://192.168.144.25:8554/video1 still opens. Ten minutes, and it decides whether the optional path on this page is real. The good news is that the required path does not depend on the answer — HDMI to the Jetson works either way.

Check your manufacturing date

A8 minis built before June 2023 may not accept 6S. If you are unsure which lot yours is from, stay on 3S or 4S. There is nothing to gain from testing it.

Parts list

The nine items from the handwritten list, each mapped to where it lands in the harness above. Item 5 is recorded with its correction intact.

01
Micro‑HDMI to HDMI cable
Camera out to capture card in. The A8 mini port is micro‑HDMI (type D), not mini‑HDMI — they are different connectors and the wrong one will not seat. Keep it short; it has to reach from the damped nose plate up to wherever the Jetson ends up, and it flexes with the gimbal.
02
Capture card
Must be UVC class so it appears as /dev/video0 with no driver, and must be USB 3.0. On USB 2.0 it will silently fall back to MJPEG, 720p or a lower frame rate instead of erroring.
03
SD card
Goes in the A8 mini for onboard recording — that is the only path that gets you actual 4K, since every live output caps at 1080p. If your Orin NX module boots from microSD rather than eMMC, you need one for that too. Buy two.
04
XT60 connector kit · XT30
The pack lead is EC5 with an XT60 adapter already on it, so XT60 is the junction standard. XT30 for the camera and Jetson branches. Buy the kit with heatshrink included.
05
Adjustable volt meter → DC–DC buck converter
Feeds the Jetson. Output voltage depends on the carrier board — commonly 5, 12 or 19 V — and that is still an open question. Size it for the Orin NX at its highest nvpmodel mode plus headroom, and confirm the carrier spec before you buy.
06
Pixhawk 6C wires
The power module and the JST‑GH looms. You need POWER1 (6‑pin, input), TELEM1 to the Jetson, a loom for the camera control port, GPS1 for the M9N and PPM/SBUS RC for the R86C. Count the pins on each loom before wiring — the connectors look alike.
07
Props — Holybro 1045 V2 (10″ × 4.5″)
The handwritten “10 45” is the 1045 size code — 10″ diameter, 4.5″ pitch — not a 4S cell count. Stock size for the X500 V2. A 10″ prop is 254 mm across, which puts its inner edge 123 mm from the frame centre; that circle is the keep‑out for anything you stack above or below the deck. Buy spares in matched CW/CCW sets.
08
USB hub / extender
The carrier board will not have enough USB 3.0 ports for the capture card plus any serial adapters. The hub must itself be USB 3.0, and if the capture card is bus‑powered, powered as well — an unpowered hub is a good way to make 1080p30 drop frames intermittently.
09
VTX for the SIYI drone
The air unit, for the optional downlink. It takes Ethernet video from the camera and sends it to the ground station or controller. Buy it last: it is the only item here whose usefulness depends on the simultaneity test above, and nothing in the required path needs it.

Bench build order

Do it in this order. Each step is testable before the next one adds a variable.

01
Use a bench supply, not a LiPo
Set 12 V, current limit 2 A. A wiring mistake trips the supply instead of melting something, and you cannot over‑discharge a pack while debugging. Save the LiPo for the airframe.
02
Power the camera alone
Nothing else connected. Confirm the three‑axis self‑test sweep, then a solid green status LED. Yellow blinking means under 10 V. This is the first time the camera has ever been powered, so do it on its own.
03
Switch the camera to HDMI output
SIYI Assistant on a Windows PC, under Gimbal Config. Out of the box it may be set to Ethernet, in which case the HDMI port is silent and the capture card sees nothing.
04
Settle the simultaneity question
With HDMI enabled, check whether rtsp://192.168.144.25:8554/video1 still opens. If it does not, the downlink option is off the table and you should not buy a VTX. The required path is unaffected either way.
05
Prove the capture path
lsusb -t (expect 5000M), then v4l2-ctl -d /dev/video0 --list-formats-ext, then ffplay /dev/video0. Record the negotiated resolution, format and frame rate — those numbers size the detector input.
06
Hand the gimbal to the Pixhawk
Wire the camera's control port to the flight controller and set up the mount driver. Confirm you can slew the gimbal from the ground station and from a stick, and that it holds level when you tilt the airframe. Do this before the Jetson is involved, so a pointing failure has only one possible cause.
07
Then the Jetson link
TELEM1 to the Jetson, bring up MAVROS, confirm GPS and attitude are arriving as topics. Only now is the harness worth moving onto the airframe — and only once you have somewhere to actually bolt the Jetson.
08
The VTX, last and only if step 4 passed
Ethernet from the camera, power from the pack, and confirm the ground station gets a picture while the Jetson is still receiving frames on /dev/video0. If either feed drops when the other is live, you have learned something important.

Still unanswered

Which carrier board is the Orin NX on? That sets the buck converter's output — 5, 12 or 19 V. Where does the Jetson physically mount? The top deck is full. ArduPilot or PX4 on the 6C? It decides the camera control port's protocol and the mount parameters. Can the camera run HDMI and Ethernet at once? That decides whether the downlink option exists. And is the Jetson allowed to write gimbal commands?
