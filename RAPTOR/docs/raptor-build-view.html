The number that decides the build

A 1045 prop is 10″ across — 254 mm. Four of them on a 500 mm wheelbase set a hard boundary on where anything can go.

Wheelbase500 mm

Motor to motor, diagonally. Each motor sits 250 mm from the frame centre.

Prop diameter254 mm

10″ × 4.5″ pitch — that is what “1045” means, and the handwritten “10 45” on the parts list is this, not a cell count.

Keep‑out radius123 mm

250 − 127. Inside this circle you can stack as tall as you like. Outside it, at prop height, nothing may exist.

Tip clearance~100 mm

Between adjacent prop tips. Comfortable — the props are nowhere near each other.

Plan view — the prop keep-out, to scale

This answers the mounting question

The earlier worry was that the Jetson has nowhere to go, because the Pixhawk and the R86C already fill the top deck. The plan view shows the way out: build upwards, not outwards. A typical Orin NX carrier is about 130 × 120 mm, whose furthest corner is only 88 mm from centre — comfortably inside the 123 mm keep‑out.

So a second deck on standoffs above the Pixhawk is clear of the props no matter how tall it is. What it costs you is centre of gravity and prop wash over the heatsink, not clearance.

Side elevation — deck order and cable lengths

The micro-HDMI run is the awkward one

It goes from the camera, which hangs below the frame on a damped plate, up to a capture card on the proposed deck above the Pixhawk — a run of roughly 180 mm of vertical travel plus slack. It must cross the vibration isolators without stiffening them, which is the whole point of the damped plate, and it must tolerate the gimbal panning underneath it.

Leave a deliberate service loop at the gimbal end, and secure the cable to the frame above the dampers, never across them.

Assembly order

Mechanical first, then power, then data. Do not reverse it.

01
Work out the second deck before anything else
Standoff height, plate size, and how the Jetson bolts to it. Everything downstream — cable lengths, buck position, CG — is set by this one decision, and it is the cheapest thing on the whole build to change now and the most expensive to change later.
02
Mount the Jetson, buck and capture card dry
No wiring. Bolt them where they will live, then check the aircraft still balances near the frame centre and that nothing intrudes past the 123 mm keep‑out at prop height.
03
Route and dress every cable before any of it is live
Lay each run, mark the length with the gimbal at full deflection and the arms folded, and only then cut. The micro‑HDMI and the camera power run together from the damped plate — sleeve them as one bundle.
04
Power, one branch at a time, on a bench supply
12 V, 2 A limit. Camera branch first and alone — solid green LED. Then the buck, with a meter on its output before the Jetson is plugged into it. Confirm the buck's set voltage against the carrier spec twice; this is the step that destroys a Jetson.
05
Camera control port to the Pixhawk
Prove you can slew the gimbal from the ground station and that it holds level when you tilt the airframe — before the Jetson is in the picture, so a pointing fault has exactly one possible cause.
06
Video, then telemetry
micro‑HDMI to the capture card, confirm /dev/video0 at 5000M. Then TELEM1 to the Jetson and MAVROS. Record the negotiated resolution and frame rate — those numbers size the detector input.
07
Balance and vibration check before props
Find the CG with the battery in. Then bench-run the motors without props and look at the FC's vibration logs — a second deck full of electronics changes the frame's resonance, and you want to know that before it is in the air.

Still unanswered

Which carrier board is the Orin NX on — that sets the buck output and the deck size. ArduPilot or PX4 on the 6C — that sets the camera control protocol. Whether the A8 mini can run HDMI and Ethernet at once — that decides if the VTX is worth buying. And whether the Jetson is allowed to write gimbal commands to the flight controller.
